### Post 3: "Why Your Competitor Ranks Above You on Google"

You offer a better service. You have been in business longer. Your reviews are better.

So why does your competitor rank above you on Google?

It is not luck. Google uses over 200 ranking factors, but for local businesses, it comes down to a handful that matter most. Let me simplify it.

**Relevance:** Does your website actually say what you do and where you do it? If your site says "quality home services" but your competitor's site says "licensed residential electrician serving Montgomery County, MD," Google knows who to show for that search.

**Proximity:** How close is your business to the searcher? You cannot change your address, but you can optimize your service area settings in Google Business Profile.

**Prominence:** How well-known and trusted is your business online? This comes down to reviews (quantity, quality, and recency), backlinks from local directories, and consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) information across the web.

**Experience:** Does your website provide a good user experience? Fast load time, mobile-friendly design, clear navigation. Google measures how users interact with your site. If visitors leave in 3 seconds, Google notices.

**Content:** Are you regularly publishing helpful content that answers the questions your customers are asking? A single FAQ page answering "How much does a roof replacement cost in [city]?" can drive dozens of calls per month.

Here is one tip you can act on today: Google your business name. Look at your Google Business Profile. Is every single field completed? Hours, services, photos, description, Q&A? An optimized profile alone can move you into the local 3-pack.

### FAQ 5: "What's the difference between SEO and paid ads?"

They serve different purposes, and the best marketing strategies typically use both. Here is the honest comparison.

**SEO is a long-term investment that compounds.** When you rank organically on Google, every click is free. It takes months to build, but once you are there, you continue attracting customers without paying per click. Over time, a well-optimized site becomes your most cost-effective marketing channel. Think of it as buying a house: it takes effort upfront, but you are building equity.

**Paid ads deliver immediate visibility.** Google Ads can put you at the top of search results today. That is powerful when you need leads now, during a seasonal push, or while your SEO is building momentum. The downside: the moment you stop paying, the leads stop. There is no residual benefit. Think of it as renting: useful, flexible, but you never own anything.

**The key differences:**

| Factor | SEO | Paid Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | 3-6 months to peak | Immediate |
| Cost per lead | Decreases over time | Stays constant or increases |
| Longevity | Lasts months/years | Stops when budget stops |
| Trust factor | Higher (organic = credibility) | Lower (users skip ads) |
| Click share | 70% of clicks go to organic | 30% go to ads |

**Our recommendation:** Start with SEO as your foundation. If you need leads immediately or are in a highly competitive market, layer in targeted paid campaigns while your organic presence builds. Once SEO is producing consistently, you can scale back ad spend and enjoy higher margins.

We manage both, and we will always be straightforward about which channel is driving results.

**Mistake #1: Your website does not mention your location.**

This sounds absurdly simple, and it is. But I audit sites every week that say things like "We provide high-quality dental services" without ever mentioning the city, county, or state they serve.

Google is not psychic. If your website does not say "dentist in Silver Spring, MD," Google will not rank you for that search.

**The fix:** Add your city and service area to your homepage title tag, H1 heading, meta description, and naturally within your page content. Do the same for every service page.

---

**Mistake #2: Your Google Business Profile is half empty.**

Your GBP is arguably more important than your website for local search. Yet most businesses fill out the basics -- name, address, phone -- and leave everything else blank.

Google treats your profile completeness as a trust and relevance signal. A profile with 30 photos, a detailed description, complete service list, regular posts, and answered Q&As will consistently outperform a bare-bones listing.

**The fix:** Log in to your Google Business Profile right now. Click through every section. Fill in every field. Upload at least 20 photos (interior, exterior, team, work examples). Write a keyword-rich description. This single action can improve your local visibility within weeks.

---

**Mistake #3: You are ignoring reviews.**

Not just having reviews. Managing them. Responding to them. Systematically generating them.

Google has confirmed that reviews are a ranking factor. Quantity, quality, recency, and your responses all matter. A business with 50 reviews that responds to every one will outrank a business with 10 unacknowledged reviews.

**The fix:** Create a review generation system. After every job or appointment, send a follow-up text or email with a direct link to your Google review page. Respond to every single review within 48 hours, positive and negative. Aim for a steady flow of 4 to 8 new reviews per month.
